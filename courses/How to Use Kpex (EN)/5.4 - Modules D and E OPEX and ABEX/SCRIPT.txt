Building a plant is only the start: it then has to be operated for decades and, at the end, decommissioned. I'm Carlos Fuenmayor, and in this lesson we close the cost modules with D and E, OPEX and ABEX, to cover the asset's whole lifecycle.

Think of it as three asset stages. Construction and installation is CAPEX, already covered by Modules A, B and C. Operation and maintenance is OPEX, Module D. And abandonment and decommissioning is ABEX, Module E. Modules D and E complete the lifecycle: they carry the same rigour from the initial investment to the end of the plant's life.

Module D, OPEX Models, holds the OPEX Estimator, in group D one: a top-down parametric estimator covering eighty facility types across nine sectors, from chemicals and energy to water and refining. You pick the facility type and its capacity —methanol, an NGCC power plant, desalination, ammonia or a refinery— and it estimates the asset's operating cost. It answers a question CAPEX never does: how much does it cost to keep this plant running year after year?

Module E, Abandonment and Decommissioning, covers the full decommissioning lifecycle, a capability that is rare in the market. It is six LIVE catalogues with a hundred and seventy-one APU in total: oil and gas onshore, in E one one, and offshore, in E one two; process plants; renewable energy; mining; and nuclear. On offshore, mining or nuclear projects, ABEX can be a very material number, so estimating it from the start changes decisions.

So how do you switch them on in an estimate? In the fifth step of the Estimate Wizard, Lifecycle Cost Scope. CAPEX is marked ALWAYS, it is always in. OPEX and ABEX are optional: switching them on brings in Modules D and E. And the Plant Facility Lifetime field appears, the plant's life in years, which Kpex uses for net present value and for discounting ABEX. With all three together you get a Lifecycle Cost Estimate, the basis for NPV and IRR analysis.
