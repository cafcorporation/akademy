We start opening Kpex's database at its finest-grained layer: the cost of a single piece of equipment. I'm Carlos Fuenmayor, and in this lesson we look at Module A, the building block of the most detailed estimates.

Module A, Process Plant Components, is the cost of the individual equipment items in a process plant: more than seven hundred parametric models of pumps, compressors, vessels, heat exchangers and conveyors. They all work the same way: you enter the item's sizing parameters, its capacity, size or power, in metric or imperial, and Kpex returns a cost range, not a single number.

The equipment is grouped by families. A one one holds the rotating equipment: compressors, pumps, turbines and fans. A one two, the static equipment: vessels, columns and tanks. Then heat transfer, material handling and process packages. But Module A does not stop at A one: from A two to A six there are their own sub-modules for piping, fire and gas, instruments, electrical and civil works. A two, piping, has seven families and more than two hundred components; A three brings its own estimators. And A seven adds the assemblies. That said: in early classes these bulks can also be estimated as allowances, a proportion of the major equipment, using the KPEX-HF factors from Module B, which we will meet in the next lesson.

Every price arrives with three properties. First, a known basis: US Gulf Coast, with a Location Factor of one, in the current month, escalated automatically on the CAF Cost Index, so you never work from a stale figure. Second, it is material-aware: the same item does not cost the same in carbon steel, stainless steel or a special alloy. And third, it is a range with statistical confidence bounds, which in an early estimate is exactly what you need: how much, and how uncertain.

Hold on to this: Module A gives you the cost of each piece of equipment, sized by you, with its material, an up-to-date basis and a confidence band. It is the building block of Kpex's most detailed estimates.

A quick exercise: pick a centrifugal pump, give it a capacity and note the range; then change the material and see how far it moves. In the next lesson we move up a level to Module B and its cost curves.
