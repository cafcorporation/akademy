The wizard creates the estimate; but what really fills it in is the workspace. I'm Carlos Fuenmayor, and in this lesson we step into that workspace: equipment, the WBS and cost line items.

Once created, an estimate opens in its own workspace. Action buttons sit at the top and, below them, four summary blocks: AACE Classification, with the class, the accuracy range and the currency; Basis of Estimate, with dates, location and notes; Cost Adjustments, the cost waterfall that runs from the base TIC, plus contingency and escalation, to the adjusted total; and WBS Cost Breakdown, the cost split along the work breakdown structure.

Look at the main buttons. Continue Estimate keeps building the current estimate and takes you to the right tool for its class: for Class 5 it opens the curve-and-capacity form, and for the other classes, the Equipment List Builder. To advance the class, though, the button is Mature to Class N, which creates the next class in the same project and carries the basis over. WBS Builder is the line-item structure builder. Edit changes the title, dates, contingency, escalation and location, but it does not touch the cost items. And Procurement shows up on Class 2 and 3 estimates, for managing packages and offers.

The Equipment List Builder works in three steps: first you define the plant areas, then you add the equipment to each area, and finally you choose the direct-cost method, using the KPEX-HF allowances or the Lang Factor. The equipment comes from the Module A catalogue, and each item goes in with its quantity and sizing parameters; the cost appears instantly, with location and escalation already applied.

All of this is organised in the WBS, the work breakdown structure, aligned with the AACE TCM Framework and the PMI WBS Standard. In the WBS Builder you build the Facility, Area, Unit and Cost Item hierarchy, and enter each line item with its quantity, unit rate and total. You can group, reorder and tag line items by discipline or by facility system; and that WBS drives the estimate summary and the reports.

And underneath it all, two engines work on their own. The CAF LFI, in Z four, moves each cost from the model's country of origin to your location. The CAF Cost Index, in Z five, escalates it to the current month. The LFI applies globally, but you can also set it line item by line item in the WBS Builder, which helps on projects that span several locations. The key idea: the wizard creates the estimate, the workspace fills it in, and the calibration runs itself.
