What if you don't have an equipment list yet, only the plant's capacity? That is what Module B is for. I'm Carlos Fuenmayor, and in this lesson we look at Kpex's cost curves, the engine for estimating at Class five and four.

Module B, Historical Benchmarks and Cost Curves, holds more than four hundred and thirty cost curves across ten sectors, validated against past projects: process plants, infrastructure, offshore oil and gas, and railway lines. It is the natural tool when you don't yet have an equipment list: all you need is the capacity.

Every curve follows a power law: total cost equals a coefficient times the capacity raised to an exponent, which we call n. The key is that n is usually below one, so total cost grows more slowly than capacity. An illustrative example: if n is zero point six and you double the capacity, the cost does not double, it rises only about fifty-two per cent, so the cost per unit falls by roughly twenty-four per cent. That is why the curves are magnitude-aware: they capture the economy of scale a flat rate ignores.

There are two curve levels. The Facility level represents a whole plant and maps one-to-one to a Module C model. The Functional Unit level represents a process unit that assembles with others into a facility. Each curve also states its origin country, so the Location Factor points the right way, and carries live escalation to the current month.

Section B two gathers cost KPIs with their statistical P ten, P fifty and P ninety range, by project type, region and decade. You use it as a reality check: if your number falls well outside the range, something deserves a second look. And remember the KPEX-HF Allowances, those bulk factors Module A uses to estimate piping, instrumentation and civil. A good example of how the modules feed each other.
