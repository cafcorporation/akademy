What if you don't have an equipment list yet, only the plant's capacity? That is what Module B is for. I'm Carlos Fuenmayor, and in this lesson we look at Kpex's cost curves, the engine for estimating at Class five and four.

Module B, Historical Benchmarks and Cost Curves, holds six hundred and fifty-three cost curves across ten sectors and sixty families, validated against past projects: process plants, infrastructure, offshore oil and gas, and railway lines. It is four tools: Cost Curves, KPI Library, Construction Costs and Benchmark Data, with its own Offshore O&G and Railway benchmarks. It is the natural tool when you don't yet have an equipment list: all you need is the capacity.

Every curve follows a power law: total cost equals a coefficient times the capacity raised to an exponent, which we call n. The key is that n is usually below one, so total cost grows more slowly than capacity. An illustrative example: if n is zero point six and you double the capacity, the cost does not double, it rises only about fifty-two per cent, so the cost per unit falls by roughly twenty-four per cent. That is why the curves are magnitude-aware: they capture the economy of scale a flat rate ignores.

There are three curve levels, lowest to highest: Component, Functional Unit and Facility. The Component is a single part or build element; the Functional Unit is a process unit that assembles with others; and the Facility level represents a whole plant, which maps one-to-one to a Module C model. Each curve also states its origin country, so the Location Factor points the right way, and carries live escalation to the current month via the Kpex Cost Index.

The KPI Library gathers cost KPIs with their statistical low, mid and high range, by project type, region and decade. You use it as a reality check: if your number falls well outside the range, something deserves a second look. And remember the KPEX-HF Allowances, the fourth tab of the KPI Library, with its P ten, P fifty and P ninety matrix: those bulk factors Module A uses to estimate piping, instrumentation and civil. A good example of how the modules feed each other.

Hold on to one line: a curve with an exponent below one says what a flat rate hides, that the bigger the project, the lower the cost per unit. Always read a KPI together with the project's magnitude. In the next lesson we move from the curve to the detail with Module C and its facility models.
