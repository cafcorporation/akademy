## Scene 3 · The curves are magnitude-aware · 1:00–1:28

- **Speaking:** Carlos · **Layout:** B
- **Background:** `screenshots/7.1-cost-curve.png`
- **On-screen text:** *exponent n < 1 → cost per unit falls* · *level: Facility · Functional Unit* · *country of origin → LFI · Cost Index*

**Script**
```
Two things worth being clear about. First, the curves are magnitude-aware: because the exponent n is below one, the cost per unit falls as the project grows. A flat rate misses that behaviour. Second, every curve has a level — a whole facility or a functional unit — and it carries its country of origin, so the Location Factor points the right way and its escalation lives on the Cost Index.
```

## Scene 4 · B.2 · P10/P50/P90 ranges · 1:28–1:58

- **Speaking:** Carlos · **Layout:** B
- **Background:** `screenshots/7.2-reports-menu.png`
- **On-screen text:** *B.2 · KPI Library* · *P10 low · P50 median · P90 high* · *reality check*

**Script**
```
The second kind of reference are the KPI ranges, in the KPI Library. They're a reality check: for each project type, region and decade they give you three numbers. P10, the low end, where only ten percent of projects cost less; P50, the median, the typical value; and P90, the high end. If your estimate lands outside the P10-to-P90 band it isn't necessarily wrong, but you should be able to explain why.
```

And to see what is actually being built around the world, there's the Global CAPEX Monitor: a public register of large industrial, energy and infrastructure projects, organised by country. You'll find it on the public website under Insights; without a membership you see a preview of the first five countries, and with an active membership, from the Practitioner tier, the full register opens.
