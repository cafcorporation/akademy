```
The CAF LFI, the Location Factor Index, adjusts any base cost to any location on earth. It covers one hundred and ninety-six countries across six continents. Its reference point is the US Gulf Coast, which equals one: the AACE standard. Above one, costlier than the Gulf Coast; below one, cheaper.
```

## Scene 3 · The formula · 0:46–1:12

- **Speaking:** Carlos · **Layout:** B
- **Background:** `screenshots/6.2-z4-pagina-pais.png`
- **On-screen text:** *E = A × (B + C + D) / 3* · *A country · B procurement · C labour · D engineering*

**Script**
```
Each country's final factor combines four components: the country's own drivers, procurement with duty and freight, labour, and engineering. Procurement, labour and engineering are averaged, and that average is multiplied by the country drivers. That's the formula you see on screen.
```

## Scene 4 · Origin to destination · 1:12–1:45

**Script**
```
And here's the subtle part. Not every model is calibrated on the Gulf Coast; some come from another country. So Kpex records each model's country of origin and applies the destination LFI divided by the origin LFI. When you pick your project country, Kpex moves the cost from its true origin to your location. The numbers in the on-screen example are made up, purely to show the mechanics.
```

## Production notes

- **Pronunciation:** set up **Kpex**, **AACE**, **CAPEX** and the spelled-out acronym **LFI** in HeyGen's dictionary. The UI names (*Location Factor*, *Basis of Estimate*, *Estimate Wizard*, *WBS Builder*) stay in English. The menu code **Z.4** is read "Z four"; on screen it stays as *Z.4*. Generate scene 4 first (the origin-to-destination formula) and listen.
- **Spell out the big numbers** ("one hundred and ninety-six countries") so the avatar reads them cleanly; on screen they stay numeric. The Gulf Coast base LFI is **1.0** ("one"), shown on screen as *1.000*; do not read it as "one thousand".
- **Example numbers:** stress on screen that they are made up; they are not real Kpex factors.
